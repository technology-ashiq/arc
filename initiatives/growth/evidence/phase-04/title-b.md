# Title template B — the specific-consequence form

The subject named with the consequence it produced, as a concrete outcome rather than a claim.

**Shape:** `<what happened> <what it cost or revealed>`

**Examples of the shape**

- A rate limit called 41 good links dead, and the run carried on
- One normalisation deleted the thing the gate was measuring

**What this template is for.** It is one of exactly two, and it exists so that
`content.published` carries a `template_id` an experiment could later read. It is
**not** an instruction to the writer about structure, length, or tone — ADR-1110
makes exemplars the only style input, and a template that started prescribing would
be the prescriptive turn arriving through the back door.

**How it differs from A.** A states the subject as a claim; B names an outcome and
what it cost. Both are one line. Neither says anything about the body of the article,
and neither may grow a rule that does.

**Honest limit.** At this cycle's volume — ten articles, five per arm — this template
and its sibling produce a *collectable* stream and **no verdict**. Evolve's per-arm
floor is roughly 1,900 trials. Anyone reading a CTR difference between the two arms
during this cycle is reading noise (ADR-1106).
