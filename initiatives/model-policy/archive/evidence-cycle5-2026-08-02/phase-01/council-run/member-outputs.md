## SKEPTIC (POINT-IDs S1…S5)

**STANCE:** AGAINST — Continuing arc-first is not cautious stewardship, it is arc's own self-diagnosed failure mode playing out in real time, against arc's own written kill-criteria, while the venture-launch trigger it set for itself passes unmet.

**S1** [High] arc's own constitution and master plan explicitly reject "arc-first" as a strategy — this isn't a competing doctrine, it's a rule being broken. A8: *"Earn before build… capability is built when a venture pulls it, never pushed by ambition"*; the master plan sets hard kill criteria (*"OS-hours > venture-hours two weeks running → OS freeze until a venture ships something"*) and names *"factory-polishing addiction"* as **the #1 real threat**; `plans/README.md:81` states *"Venture track outweighs OS track on any tie."* No document anywhere argues arc-first as a principle (F1, F5, F6, F7, F8).

**S2** [High] The venture-launch trigger arc set for itself is being missed as this decision is being made, not hypothetically in the future. `PLAN-cycle3-venture-launch.md` fixes the trigger at ~2 weeks after Cycle 2 closes; Cycle 2 started 2026-07-22 and is still LIVE, placing the trigger at ≈2026-08-05 — **three days out**. Four arc-internal cycles were inserted ahead of Cycle 3's slot, three have already run, and arc's own history log records the venture decision as *"decision overdue"* (F13, F14, F15, F18).

**S3** [High] Zero revenue, zero live venture, ~16-20 days of elapsed calendar time already spent on six internal initiatives while the founder is explicitly time- and revenue-pressured with nothing shipped externally (F16, F17, F12). Combined with two well-established, widely-cited engineering failure modes that describe exactly this pattern — Spolsky's "architecture astronaut" (building the general template instead of the actual problem) and Brooks' "second-system effect" (the second system absorbs every deferred frill and ships late) — the shape of what's happening matches named failure patterns even though no direct empirical study of "solo founder, tooling-first" exists to statistically prove it (F21, F22; caveated by F34).

**S4** [High] arc's own plan library shows the "good shape" goal as stated is structurally unreachable without a venture: of 17 plans carrying a pull trigger, **zero are buildable with no precondition at all**, and 6 explicitly require a live venture or revenue. This cuts against my own case in one direction (it's evidence arc genuinely has work that can't be pulled without ventures existing) but it also cuts against arc-first: it means "finish arc to self-standing good shape, then launch a venture" is not a coherent two-step sequence — a third of arc's own remaining plan library cannot even be *started*, let alone finished, until a venture exists to pull it. Waiting for arc to be "done" before starting a venture defers the very trigger arc says it needs (F10).

**S5** [Med] Startup Genome's retrospective study of ~3,200 startups found "premature scaling" — including investing in scalability before product/market fit — was the most-cited failure cause, present in ~70% of studied startups, with 93% of those never reaching $100k/month. This is adjacent evidence (scaling an existing product early, not building infrastructure with zero product), and its primary source could not be rendered this session (confirmed only via secondary citations) — flagged Med, not leaned on as a hard number (F23).

**STRONGEST:** This isn't a case built on outside analogy or unsettled psychology research — it's that arc's own written, self-imposed kill criteria (F5, F6, F7) and its own self-set venture trigger (F13/F14, landing in 3 days) are being missed *while this very decision is being deliberated*, and arc's own history log already calls the venture decision "overdue" (F18). The strongest argument against continuing arc-first is that arc already told itself not to, in writing, before any of this pressure existed — and the decision under review is asking whether to keep overriding that self-warning for a fifth cycle.

**BIGGEST UNCERTAINTY:** F34/F35 are fatal to any claim that arc-first will *statistically* fail: no study directly compares solo founders who build tooling before revenue against those who ship first, and my only concrete counter-cases (Slack, Trello, Rails, Shopify) are survivors — there's no visible denominator for internal tools that were built alongside nothing and simply died. I cannot show arc-first usually fails; I can only show it matches named qualitative failure patterns and violates arc's own stated rules. Separately, F10's finding that 6 of 17 plans need a live venture is real leverage for the Advocate: if any of the four inserted cycles (especially the still-LIVE Receipt Spine, which the revenue-event mechanism in F19 may depend on) turn out to be genuine prerequisites for capturing the venture's first `revenue.received` event, then some of this "arc-first" work isn't drift — it's load-bearing infrastructure the venture literally cannot launch without.

**IF I'M WRONG:** If Ashiq or the council can show that Cycle 2's Receipt Spine (still LIVE, F11) is a hard technical dependency for recording Cycle 3's *"first real `revenue.received` event"* (F19) — i.e., that the venture cannot even measure its own launch success without it — that would convert my "trigger is being missed" argument into "the trigger's own precondition wasn't ready," which meaningfully weakens the case that continuing arc-first is undisciplined. Similarly, if the four inserted cycles (design, portfolio, model-policy, develop) were each explicitly and individually sign-off'd by Ashiq with a stated reason tied to venture-launch readiness — rather than accreting as sequential internal-facing work — that would reframe the delay as deliberate risk management rather than the "factory-polishing addiction" arc warned itself about.
