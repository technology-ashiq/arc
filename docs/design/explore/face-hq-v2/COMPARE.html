arc face — six directions on the open brief

The previous round scored 18/100. Its brief specified the look; these six were given a brief that specifies only what is true and what would be a lie, and hands everything else to the designer.

Scroll each panel. My own scores are below each one, and I have opened every render rather than reading a report about it. No single one of these is 90 yet, and the last block says what I would do about that.

RANK 1 · my score ~85 · lens: subtraction
open-d
Minutes of an automatic company. Cream, one blue accent, hairlines, no cards at all.
Its win: the second sentence. "Thirteen decisions are waiting on you. Nothing else needs a human today." It tells you what you do NOT have to do. The reason field is the hero and its placeholder reads "arc will not draft it". The 13 are grouped 1/3/7/2 by kind, not listed as 13 cards. And the sharpest honest line anyone wrote: "every green figure on this page is money going out".
Weak spot: the lane spend row is cramped, and it has no single arresting object.
RANK 2 · my score ~82 · lens: editorial
open-f
The company's paper of record. Serif, printer's marks, a manicule pointing at what needs a hand.
Its win: the best headline of the six. "Twelve can wait. One cannot." That triages the queue instead of counting it. And the zero is drawn hollow: "it prints hollow because the event has never fired, not because the amount is little. The day it fires, this line prints filled, here first, in this ink and no other."
Weak spot: a real text collision at the bottom of the page. Fixable, but it is a bug.
RANK 3 · my score ~80 · lens: sovereign instrument
fable-a
Dark, ceremonial. One warm light, rising from the thing that needs you.
Its win: the decision ring, and it is the best single object anyone drew. 55 ticks, 42 quiet, 13 burning, and the gap left open at the bottom because the record is not closed. The morning's whole arithmetic as one thing. Also: "HELD APART, never on the tape" for the 245 refused records, none counted and none thrown away.
Weak spot: the left rail is doing four jobs, and the 2nd/3rd approvals are thin rows.
RANK 4 · my score ~78 · lens: editorial ledger
fable-b
The operating record. Warm paper, broadsheet masthead, a 22 day receipt stave.
Its win: revenue set as the single italic word "Absent", and beneath it an empty green ruled box: "green ink is reserved. The first real rupee prints here." Green appears nowhere else on the page, so its arrival will mean something. Also a real rubber stamp: DECIDED BY HUMAN HAND, NEVER BY MACHINE.
Weak spot: busiest left rail of the six; the lower approvals feel unfinished.
RANK 5 · my score ~70 · lens: total freedom
open-c
Operations deck. Dark, four status tiles, the most conventional of the six.
Its win, and it is a real one: the event kinds drawn as a lamp panel. 46 lamps, 14 lit, and one outlined in green among the dark ones with the caption "revenue.received waits among the dark: the lamp the whole company exists to light".
Weak spot: card dashboard layout, and the tape at the bottom has overlapping labels.
RANK 6 · my score ~55 · lens: living instrument
open-e
Cockpit. The densest by far, and the only one that broke its frame.
Its win: "THREE ZEROS, TOLD APART" states the hardest constraint outright, and the per kind bar chart with real counts is genuinely useful.
Weak spot: it overflows to 2410px wide against a 1440 frame, and the top badges collide. Density taken past legibility is not density, it is clutter.

What I actually think

None of these is 90 on its own. But the best moments are spread across four of them, and they are not in conflict, because each solves a different part of the same screen:

  • open-d's structure and voice: the second sentence, the reason field as the hero, the queue grouped by kind rather than listed, and the three kinds of zero drawn as three distinct marks.
  • open-f's triage headline and its hollow zero.
  • fable-a's decision ring, which is the arresting object open-d is missing.
  • open-c's unlit green lamp: the one event the whole company exists to fire.

Those four together are a 90. The reason they are separable is that the open brief let each designer solve the problem from a different end, instead of four people rendering one person's taste.

Every figure in all six pages is real: 1,150 receipts, 13 open approvals, 14 of 46 kinds fired, 7 live lanes, and a revenue event that has never once happened.
