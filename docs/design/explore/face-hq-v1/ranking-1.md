# Ranking — juror 1

- ranked: item-3 > item-1 > item-2 > item-4
- reference-position: unset

## Why item-3 over item-1

- Both render the fact pack's *corrected* spine total — "1,146 receipts · 21 day-closed
  seals · 14 of 46 kinds have ever fired" — so this pair ties on the exercise's central
  honesty test. But item-3 carries that discipline on the near-black ink surface the
  brief names as primary ("ink surfaces (near-black) with a paper mode for printing the
  brief"), while item-1 renders every screen, Today through Board, on the cream/paper
  surface the brief scopes to printing. The result reads like a printed dossier, not
  "the cockpit of a company you can trust with your eyes closed."
- The Map: item-3 renders seven lane rows as a scannable table — a progress track, a
  phase marker, a status dot per lane — with a legend row of station-state icons beneath
  it. That is close to Vignelli's "every line distinguishable at a glance." item-1's Map
  is a circular hub-and-spoke diagram with connector lines crossing toward a center
  point — busier to trace, closer to a network diagram than a transit map.
- Density and type: item-3's tables (Spine/Tape kind counts, Board) hold tight, tabular
  monospace-numeral columns across a genuinely dense page, matching the reference bar's
  Linear callout (density + calm). item-1 leans on a uniform bordered box around every
  section to do the separating work that type scale should be doing, which flattens the
  hierarchy and reads more like a form than an instrument.
- Ask arc: item-3 shows an answered query ("why is growth blocked?") with a grounded,
  receipt-cited answer sitting on the page. item-1's Ask arc is a bare input box with a
  thin one-line answer beneath it — less demonstrated grounding for the same feature.

## Why item-1 over item-2

- Fact accuracy is the deciding line. item-1's Spine/Tape subtitle reads "1,146
  receipts... 14 of 46 kinds have ever fired" — the door-derived, corrected figure.
  item-2's same subtitle, in the largest numeral on that screen, reads "1,386" — the
  number the fact pack explicitly flags as wrong ("this pack first carried 1,386... The
  door's own spineHealth() says 1,146... the whole argument for deriving every number
  instead of quoting one"). That is exactly the slop-kill-list failure mode the brief
  names: a stale number sitting where a real one belongs.
- Honesty classing is intact on both: real ₹0 kept apart from a separately
  hatched-violet SIMULATED panel, refusal codes shown verbatim, costs listed but never
  totalled. On E3 discipline the two are close; the wrong headline total is what
  separates them.
- Approve/reject: both keep REASON as a labelled, mandatory field directly under the
  decision detail, with APPROVE/REJECT as the card's only two buttons — a clean, low-risk
  single decision either way. This pair is otherwise close; the stale total is the tie-
  breaker.

## Why item-2 over item-4

- Both carry the same stale "1,386 receipts" figure, so this specific defect is a wash
  between them — except item-4 states it twice, and the first time is inside the page's
  opening sentence: "19 Aug 2026, IST — 1,386 receipts on the spine — 21 day-closed
  seals · 14 of 46 kinds have ever fired." The wrong number is the very first fact the
  page asserts, in prose, before any card or table.
- The Map: item-2 renders an actual visual — seven horizontal lane rows, each with a
  phase-position marker and a status dot, plus a line reading "7 lanes in flight... full
  picture, one glance → Map." item-4's Map section is a paragraph of prose plus a flat
  list of coloured dots (bench · engine · face · growth · leads · legal · scheduler) with
  no diagram, chart, or spatial layout. Map is one of the brief's 8 named signature
  screens and its explicit bar is Vignelli's subway map — rendering it as text only, with
  nothing to actually scan, is a real gap on that screen specifically.
- Always-visible elements: item-2 shows a literal ring near the top of the page ("16
  lanes LIVE vs guideline 2 — informational") — a direct, legible answer to the brief's
  "arc ring" requirement. item-4 has no equivalent ring or dial visible anywhere on the
  page.
- Ask arc: item-2 shows explicit command chips ("/arc-develop next --lane face",
  "/arc-phase-done 03 --lane face") under a "select all that apply" label — a literal,
  well-formed answer to "chips for run-it-yourself." item-4's Ask arc shows only a
  question/answer pair with a Seal note; no chip affordance is visible.

## What would change my mind

item-4's Inbox/Today treatment is the deepest of the four on the brief's central
decision: a single full-profile approval card, a real receipt-drawer excerpt (kind,
ULID, lane, gate, profile fields, `supersedes: null`), an explicit "Seals" callout that
spells out in plain language why the stamp is the only write the screen can make
("Approving does not publish... a separate, forever-human keystroke arc cannot
perform"), and a literal keyboard-first legend (`j/k` · `a/r` · `w` · `t` · `/`) sitting
at the very top of the page. If that same depth were paired with the corrected receipt
total and an actual drawn Map instead of a text paragraph, item-4 would move to the top
of this ranking outright — the decision-screen craft is already there; the surrounding
data discipline and one signature screen are what is missing.
