# Ranking — juror 2

- ranked: item-1 > item-3 > item-2 > item-4
- reference-position: unset

## Why item-1 over item-3
- Item-1 gives the eye somewhere to rest: numbered section dividers, generous vertical
  spacing, and a short plain-language sentence under each header (e.g. under TODAY)
  before the dense tables start. Item-3 runs all eight sections — Today through Ask arc —
  edge-to-edge at the same tight small-caps rhythm with almost no breathing room between
  them. Worked 30-60 minutes a day for a year, item-3's unbroken density is the higher
  fatigue risk of the two.
- Item-1's headline Spine figure reads as 1,146 receipts, matching the fact pack's
  corrected count, and its per-kind breakdown (note.logged 918, approval.requested 55,
  decision.recorded 42…) reads consistent with the same source — the numbers a returning
  user re-checks every morning read as trustworthy.
- Item-3 renders its data-mode "LIVE" badge in the same green the brief reserves for
  money-real figures. Over a year of scanning by color alone, that's one of only two
  greens on the page no longer meaning one single thing.
- This is close: item-3's Map is the more scalable artifact of the two, with chain / live
  / sim / rehearsal / drill and incident / quiet-lane rendered as first-class checkbox
  filters on the lane rows rather than only a color legend — a design that keeps working
  the same way at ten times the lane count. Item-1's radial "orbit" Map (concentric rings,
  hub-and-spoke labels) is the more novel read once, but angular label crowding is the
  kind of thing that degrades faster than parallel rows as more lanes get added.

## Why item-3 over item-2
- Item-3's Spine/Tape and Board tables read as internally consistent with the fact pack's
  per-kind counts. Item-2's Spine/Tape headline stat reads "1,386 receipts" — the exact
  figure the fact pack names as the mistaken number, superseded by the corrected 1,146 —
  on the one page whose entire premise is "every number traceable." A wrong headline
  number on the receipts total is the specific failure this brief's kill-list exists to
  prevent, and it is the first big stat a returning user would re-check.
- Item-3 bakes the E3 honesty classes into first-class filter checkboxes on the Map itself
  (chain / live / sim / rehearsal / drill, incident / quiet lane) rather than relying on a
  single ring widget. Item-2's ring ("16 lanes LIVE vs guideline 2") is legible today, but
  a circular gauge comparing a count against a guideline risks being misread as a
  completion percentage on a quick glance — a small daily legibility tax the brief's
  "informational" label doesn't fully cancel out.
- Item-3's Ask arc panel shows a real answered example ("why is growth blocked?") with a
  cited-evidence reply already in view, demonstrating the Why?-opens-receipts principle
  live on the page rather than only as an empty search box.

## Why item-2 over item-4
- Item-2 persists a sidebar with the needs-you count, the LIVE/REPLAY/SIM toggle, and the
  lane-count ring always in view, and lets a returning user jump straight to Inbox or Map.
  That is the daily 30-60-minute triage habit the brief is asking for. Item-4 is one long
  editorial scroll with no persistent chrome — every return visit re-starts at the top of
  a narrative rather than at what changed.
- Item-4 carries the same stale "1,386 receipts" figure as item-2, but states it even more
  prominently — in the first paragraph under Today, the very first thing read each
  morning, rather than inside a stat card further down the page. Same trust defect,
  worse placement.
- Item-4's Today view spends most of the fold on a single open approval's full case file
  (pair grid, receipt-drawer JSON, seals note, reason box) before the rest of the queue
  appears. That is a rich read for that one record, but the fact pack's real inbox has 13
  open approvals across four profiles — a single-record-per-screen pattern will not scale
  to a fast multi-item morning scan the way item-2's stacked, same-height approval cards
  do.
- Item-4 reduces the Map to a paragraph plus a row of colored name-chips — the thinnest
  visual treatment of "the Map" among all four items, for a surface the brief names as one
  of its three explicit reference bars (Vignelli). Harder to scan for "what changed" at a
  glance than any of the other three items' Map.

## What would change my mind
Item-4's single-approval detail treatment is the most complete, literal answer to the
brief's "disclosed: room → station → receipt drawer (canonical JSON, sha, supersedes
chain)" clause of any item — the full JSON receipt drawer, the seal note explaining what a
seal cannot do, and pre-set reason chips ("evidence sufficient," "preview broken," "needs
revision") are real, working answers to clause 3's mandatory-reason requirement that none
of the other three items build out this completely. If that single record view sat behind
a fast multi-item list rather than occupying the whole Today fold, and if its headline
receipts figure matched the corrected count, it would be a serious contender for the top
spot instead of the bottom.
