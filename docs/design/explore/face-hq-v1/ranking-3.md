# Ranking — juror 3

- ranked: item-3 > item-4 > item-1 > item-2
- reference-position: unset

## Why item-3 over item-4
- Item-3's Inbox splits the "ready-to-decide queue, most at risk first" from the already-
  decided history and says outright that the rest are accounted for "by construction, not
  by hiding" — an active sentence doing the brief's honesty work ("every state names its
  evidence or its absence"), not a quiet omission the reader has to trust.
- Item-3's Map renders each lane as a compact status row with a visible "zero receipts
  stations render dashed" callout and highlights the growth → cluster station transition —
  density with purpose (the kill-list's "a chart that decorates instead of answers"),
  closer to the Vignelli bar of instant legibility than a purely illustrative diagram.
- Item-3 keeps its LIVE/REPLAY/SIM control, per-lane clocks and appetite/burn figures
  legible at high density as one continuous read rather than disconnected panels — closer
  to "a morning triage without touching the mouse" (answer 7).
- Item-4 undercuts its own careful honesty rhetoric on the one number that matters most:
  its opening sentence states the spine total as "1,386 receipts," and the Spine/Tape
  section repeats the same figure as its headline stat. That is precisely the number the
  fact pack disowns — "this pack first carried 1,386... the door's own spineHealth() says
  1,146... the whole argument for deriving every number instead of quoting one." An item
  whose lead sentence reproduces the retracted number is failing the exact test the brief
  built into the content.

## Why item-4 over item-1
- Item-4 renders the seal as prose, not just an icon: a labelled "Seal" block explains that
  the stamp is the only write the system can make, and that approving does not publish —
  publishing is a separate, heavier act arc cannot perform. That is the slop-kill-list's
  "button styled onto a forever-human act... the UI's honesty about what it cannot do IS
  the design," enacted as visible content rather than merely avoided by omission.
- Item-4's featured approval opens a full receipt drawer with raw JSON, a kind line, a
  hash-shaped line and a supersedes chain — a rendered instance of answer 5's disclosure
  layer ("room → station → receipt drawer: canonical JSON, sha, supersedes chain"). Item-1's
  cards stop at title + reason field + approve/reject, with no comparable drawer anywhere
  in the set of cards shown.
- Item-4 adds a qualifying line on its featured card — that no prior decision.recorded
  exists for this gate, and today's is the kickoff gate, not the review-pack gate — closing
  a misread that item-1 leaves open between its two same-styled "growth gate" Today cards.
- Item-1's Today cards route the decision through a "STAMP AS DECIDED" link rather than
  exposing approve/reject with the mandatory reason field inline; the CLI's actual verbs and
  the mandatory reason (answer 3) only appear once you leave Today for Inbox — one more
  click away from the "decide it with a reason" promise on the page you land on first.

## Why item-1 over item-2
- Item-2's Spine/Tape section headlines "1,386" as its hero receipts count — the exact
  figure the fact pack retracted in favour of the derived 1,146. Item-1 does not commit to
  a headline total it can't stand behind; its per-kind counts sit in a table with no
  asserted (and wrong) hero number.
- Item-2's Inbox summary line reads "55 raised, 42 decided, 2 open" — arithmetically that
  leaves 13 open, not 2, and 2 is precisely the stale earlier reading the fact pack names
  outright ("the count was 2 in an earlier snapshot and nobody noticed it becoming 13...
  this is the argument for the room"). Reproducing that stale count in the room built to
  prevent exactly this is the fact pack's own worked failure case landing on the page.
- Item-1's Inbox cards carry a mandatory "REASON — MANDATORY" field with placeholder copy
  and paired APPROVE/REJECT buttons inline on the card itself, satisfying answer 3's
  "mandatory typed reason" without a second screen.
- Item-2 does have real credit — a persistent LIVE/REPLAY/SIM switch and an arc-ring widget
  match answer 5's always-visible list more literally than item-1's single static "LIVE"
  badge — but two independent stale numbers on the same page, both matching the fact
  pack's own cautionary examples almost exactly, outweigh that structural credit under a
  hostile read.

## What would change my mind
Item-2's structural bones — sidebar navigation, a literal always-on LIVE/REPLAY/SIM switch,
and an arc-ring widget — are the most literal on-page answer to clause 5's "always visible"
list of any of the four; if its two headline numbers actually read 1,146 and 13 rather than
1,386 and 2, its layout discipline alone could beat item-1's. Symmetrically, if item-4's
spine total reads 1,146 rather than 1,386 on a closer pass than a scan this dense allows,
its seal-prose and receipt-drawer disclosure are strong enough to deserve first place over
item-3 — that single figure is the whole gap between them.
