# Ranking — juror 3

- ranked: variant-a > variant-c > variant-b

## Why variant-a over variant-c

Both keep every pending decision's verdict, ₹ amount, and kill-criteria state readable
without a click — the brief's hardest rule (answer 4) — but variant-a answers the brief's
explicit structural ask more literally. It shows two clearly labeled, always-visible panels
— "Approval inbox" on the left and "Today's event timeline" on the right — which is the
named split the brief calls for. Variant-c folds settled, loading, unresolved and undecided
events into one long chronological log; its "skim — undecided only" sidebar helps you find
what needs action, but those skim rows are bare one-liners (venture + state label only, no
amount, no kill-criteria) — you still return to the main log to see the three mandatory
facts, so the "inbox" isn't its own peer surface the way the brief frames it.

Variant-a also separates the day's pending work from what's already settled: seven rows in
the inbox, a short "Done today" list below it. That means clearing the stack means reading
one focused block, not scanning past interleaved settled/loading lines to find the next
open item. And its "state matrix — reference" block at the foot of the page is explicitly
marked "NOT LIVE DATA," so the demonstration of empty/loading/disabled states never gets
confused with the real inbox above it; variant-c's state legend mixes a fabricated "EMPTY
(EXAMPLE)" row into what otherwise reads as live status counts, which blurs that line
slightly.

Both execute the destructive-action rule the same way — "Kill Vistara Mobility — halts
funding — unrecoverable" versus "Kill Dilli Dosa Co — halts all spend and autonomy" — and
both write specific, actionable inline failures naming what failed and the next step. This
is close on the actual decision-card content; the gap is in how cleanly each names and
separates its two mandatory always-visible surfaces.

## Why variant-c over variant-b

Variant-c keeps the event timeline as the dominant, always-visible surface, and every
pending line in it — NeoKirana, Chai Point Express, Dilli Dosa Co, Bombay Bites, Aharam
Meals — carries its verdict/score, its ₹ amount and its kill-criteria signal in that one
line, satisfying "one line per timeline event" and "never behind a click" at once. Variant-b
reduces every queued item except the one currently opened to a venture name and a bare
amount in the left rail — Saathi Health, Vahaan Logistics, Naaptol Foods and the rest show
no verdict, no score, no kill-criteria until you click in, which is the exact thing answer 4
rules out for five of variant-b's six open items.

Variant-b's own "today's events" log is a three-line footnote under the queue rail, never
shown carrying more than a plain settled line — against the brief's KPI-row/timeline/inbox
trio being equally always-visible, the timeline reads like an afterthought here, not a peer.
Variant-b's printed keyboard legend also lists "j k rail" for moving between queue items and,
separately, "k" for kill — the same key doing two jobs in the very legend meant to sell the
"faster once learned" keyboard path, which undercuts that promise on the page itself.

Variant-b is not without a real strength: its single-item workspace goes deeper than either
other variant on one decision — a numeric kill-criteria table (runway/burn/margin/MRR-growth
each with a floor and a status), a four-receipt history for the venture, and reason chips
that are the clearest on-screen proof of "reject with a reason prompt" anywhere in the set.
That depth is real, but it costs a full page of reading per decision, which works against a
brief whose stated job is clearing a stack of decisions in minutes, not studying one.

## What would change my mind

Variant-c stages "return after interruption" (answer 6) more vividly than variant-a does:
its banner — "Position in the day: event 3 of 10 — resuming at the earliest unresolved
event... ordered newest-first below; nothing lost" — plus the explicit "RESUMING HERE"
marker and "SINCE YOU LEFT — newest first" section header, actually performs the scenario
the brief describes. Variant-a only states the behavior in a caption ("re-orders
newest-first on return, nothing lost") without showing the moment. If variant-a paired its
cleaner inbox/timeline split with a resume marker as concrete as variant-c's, the gap
between them would close further — this, plus variant-c's amounts reading flush-right where
variant-a's read left-set in their column, are the strongest things the trailing variants do
that the winner doesn't.
