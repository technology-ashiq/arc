# Ranking — juror 1

- ranked: variant-a > variant-c > variant-b

## Why variant-a over variant-c

- **All seven pending decisions carry their three required facts at once, with nothing behind a click.** A's approval table shows verdict+score, ₹ amount, and kill-criteria state as columns on every row simultaneously — a single glance covers the whole inbox. C reaches the same per-row transparency but buries it inside one long chronological list that also carries settled history (Kadamba Foods, Suvidha Grocers) and "later today, not yet disclosed" placeholder rows before/around the actionable items, so clearing the day's decisions costs more scrolling and reading-past-noise than A's job of "see everything, clear decisions in under ten minutes" wants.
- **Verdict formatting is unambiguous in A, murky in C.** A writes "Promote 8.4", "Kill 3.2" — a clear score on a clear scale. C compresses to "promote 84", "hold 52" with no decimal and no visible scale anchor, which reads terse but tips toward the brief's anti-word "vague" — a user has to guess whether 84 is a percentage, a score-out-of-100, or something else.
- **The state matrix is demonstrated more completely and more legibly.** A has a labeled "STATE MATRIX — REFERENCE, NOT LIVE DATA" block showing loading and empty for the inbox and loading/empty/disabled for the timeline as real dashed mockups, on top of live error (NeoKirana) and disabled (Arthveda) rows in the working inbox itself. C gets close — SETTLED/UNRESOLVED/LOADING/DISABLED all appear live in its combined list — but its "empty" state is only a one-line text mention in a sidebar legend, not a rendered reference, and because C fuses timeline and inbox into one surface it's less clear the brief's "per surface" requirement (inbox AND timeline each declare all five) is actually being met twice rather than once.
- **A's on-demand disclosure maps precisely onto the brief's named split.** Selecting Suvidha Health reveals exactly the two things the brief names as on-demand — evidence receipts and autonomy-ladder detail ("L1→L2 requires MRR≥₹4,00,000 for 2 consecutive months... 2 of 2 met") — while the always-visible row keeps only the three required facts. C's per-row evidence toggle is a fine analogue, but it has no equivalent surfaced autonomy-ladder mechanic anywhere in the visible page.

This is not a close call — C is a competent, literal execution of "return after interruption" (its resume banner and "since you left — newest first" section are a strong, almost verbatim answer to that clause) but its merged-surface density and vaguer scores put real distance between it and A.

## Why variant-c over variant-b

- **Kill-criteria and verdict are visible for every item, not just the one currently open.** C's chronological rows each carry verdict, amount, and kill-criteria state inline. B only shows this triad in full for whichever single queue item is selected — the other five queue entries (Saathi Health, Vahaan Logistics, Naaptol Foods, Trestle Analytics, Bazaarly) show only a name and an amount in the rail, so a user must click into each one before they can see the verdict or kill-criteria state the brief says must be visible "on the card, never behind a click." That is a direct, citable gap against the brief's own wording, and it sits on the object the brief calls primary.
- **B is calm and factual but not dense.** The single-decision detail pane (verdict card, amount card, a four-metric kill-criteria card, a four-row prior-receipts comparison table) spends a lot of vertical space reviewing one venture; the brief's three feel words are calm · dense · factual, and B leans hard on the first and third at the expense of the second. C's one-line-per-event rows are visibly tighter.
- **B's timeline is a footnote; C's is the spine of the page.** B's "Today's events" log is a cramped four-line list wedged under the queue rail with no error/loading/disabled variation shown anywhere. The brief requires the timeline to declare all five states just as the inbox does — C's merged surface demonstrates most of them live; B effectively skips this for its timeline entirely.
- **B does have one genuine edge C lacks:** the reject/kill action row shows reason chips ("Evidence insufficient", "Kill-criteria breached", "Runway below floor"...) sitting visibly on the card, which is the clearest concrete answer any of the three variants gives to "reject with a reason prompt... selected from presets, no free typing." C shows no equivalent affordance anywhere in view. That is a real point in B's favour and keeps this pairing closer than the first.

## What would change my mind

Variant-b's visible reason-preset chips are the one thing on any losing variant that variant-a plainly lacks — a's Reject buttons show no evidence of the "reason required, selected from presets" mechanic anywhere in the render. If a's actual reject flow doesn't implement that (rather than it simply not being caught in a static, no-interaction capture), that is a real content-contract gap, and enough evidence of it would move variant-b up.
