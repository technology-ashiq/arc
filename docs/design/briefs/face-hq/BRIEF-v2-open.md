# arc face — the open brief

> **Why this brief is short.** The first one was long and specified the look: "cold ink
> monochrome, dense, keyboard-first, one surface, no routes, these tokens." Four designers
> built exactly that and the owner scored it **18/100** — "very basic, LKG design, boring
> tech". The composers did not fail. The brief did: it spent its words on aesthetics, which
> are the designer's job, and skimped on meaning, which is the only thing a brief is for.
>
> So this one tells you **what is true** and **what would be a lie**, and then gets out of
> the way. Everything not written here is yours to invent, including things the last round
> treated as settled.

---

## Who this is for

One person. He runs a company whose employees are machines. He has **30 to 60 minutes a
day** for it, and in that time he must know what happened, decide what only he can decide,
and leave. He is not a manager reading reports. He is the one human in a system that keeps
running whether he looks or not.

He is technical, impatient, and allergic to decoration that carries no information. He also
looked at four competent, rule-abiding screens and called them kindergarten work — so
"clean and functional" is the floor, not the goal.

## The one job

**Open it. See what is true. Decide what needs him. Leave.**

If your design makes that faster, calmer or clearer than a well-made list would, it is
working. If it is beautiful and he still has to hunt, it is not.

---

## What is true right now (real data, use it verbatim)

- **13 `approval.requested` are open and waiting on him.** 55 have been raised, 42 decided.
  The difference is his queue. Four kinds of thing are waiting: a phase closure
  (`01M05A4Y4QM54VRQWSNE9ENQMD`, "approve moving past phase 04 in the engine lane — the
  day-5 kill checkpoint is 0.5 days away with REQ-02 uncertified", raised 4 days ago and the
  oldest open), three drafts needing a verdict (`01M0B663APQMV49EQR9AGR1WJQ`, "accept or
  reject build-in-public draft 1 of 3"), seven requests to escalate a process to a stronger
  model tier (`01M0ASG8JG8X6PQ0045MJ6TY8Q`), and two content gates.
- **Real revenue is ₹0.** Not "low" — *absent*. The event that records money has never
  fired once. The company is proven to work and has never earned. Rendering that as a sad
  empty state would be a lie in the other direction; it is a fact he already knows and has
  decided to live with.
- **The log holds 1,150 receipts across 22 days**, 21 of them sealed shut. Every number
  anywhere in this product comes from that log and can be traced back to the exact receipt
  that produced it.
- **Only 14 of the 46 kinds of event have ever happened.** Two thirds of the machinery is
  built, tested, and has never run in anger. That is not zero. It is *unexercised*, and the
  distinction matters more than almost anything else on the screen.
- **16 lanes of work exist; 7 are live**: bench, engine, face, growth, leads, legal,
  scheduler. Each has a phase number and a budget it is burning.
- Today so far: a decision recorded, an approval raised, a coverage check passed, an article
  published.

## What would be a lie (these cannot bend, and each one is meaning, not taste)

1. **A machine may never decide.** Exactly one action in this entire product writes
   anything, and it is a human approving or rejecting, with a reason he typed himself. No
   bulk action, no default reason, no undo. Everything else a person might want to do is
   either a command *he* runs (show him the exact one) or something no one may automate (say
   which rule forbids it, and mean it).
2. **Real, simulated, rehearsal and drill data are different substances.** They are never
   added together, never averaged, never shown in a way that lets the eye mistake one for
   another.
3. **A number with no receipt behind it may not appear.** No estimates, no projections, no
   "about". If he cannot get from a number to the thing that caused it, cut the number.
4. **Never print a bare `0` that could mean two things.** "Has never happened" and "we do
   not measure this" must be visibly different states, and both must be visibly different
   from "measured, and the answer is zero".
5. **The system's own words are used exactly.** `approval.requested` is called
   `approval.requested`. Prettifying arc's vocabulary into product-speak breaks the one
   thing that lets him trust what he reads.
6. **Four signals must be unmistakable at a glance and must never be borrowed for
   decoration:** *needs a human* · *real money* · *something is wrong* · *this is not real
   data*. **Which visual language carries them is entirely yours.** The last round used
   amber/green/red/hatched-violet; you are under no obligation to.

## What is yours

Everything else. To be explicit, because the last brief pre-decided all of it:

- The metaphor. The structure. Whether this is one screen or several.
- Light or dark. Whether there is a sidebar, or a rail, or neither.
- Typography, scale, density, rhythm, colour, material, depth, motion.
- What the primary object even is. The last round assumed "a queue of cards". It might be a
  map, an instrument, a document, a timeline, a room, a single sentence.
- Whether a dashboard is the right answer at all. **If you think it is not, build the thing
  you think is right and say why.**

## The bar

The owner expected world-class and got kindergarten. Assume he has seen every SaaS
dashboard. What earns 90 out of 100 is craft he can feel in the first two seconds and
respect after an hour: real hierarchy, real light, decisions about what *not* to show, and
one moment that makes him want to keep it open.

Research what excellence looks like right now before you begin. Do not copy it. Take the
principles and build something that could only be arc.

**One more thing.** Every previous attempt looked like software for looking at data. This is
software for *commanding a company*. That should feel different.
