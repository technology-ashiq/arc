KEY REASONS:
- [N2] **"Good shape" as defined cannot be reached on the arc-first path.** 6 of the 17 pull-triggered plans (growth, leads, ops, legal-pack, ledger, trader) require a live venture or revenue to be buildable at all. If "strategy plans implemented" includes those six — and the definition says it does — then arc-first has a target it is structurally incapable of hitting. The plan does not merely take long; it does not terminate.
- [S1] **arc's own operative doctrine already says the opposite, in a non-draft document.** `plans/README.md:81`: *"Venture track outweighs OS track on any tie."* The master execution plan names *"factory-polishing addiction"* as **the #1 real threat** and sets an OS-freeze rule against it. No document anywhere argues arc-first as a principle — the arc-side sequencing is the accumulated result of individually-approved insertions, not a stated doctrine competing with this one.
- [N3] **Throughput on a non-venture target is still off-target.** The execution pace is genuinely high, and that is not in dispute; but none of the closed cycles was the venture cycle, four internal cycles were inserted ahead of Cycle 3's slot, and the revenue target predates those insertions. Speed does not convert into progress toward the stated priority.
- [S4] **The arc-first sequence is self-defeating on its own terms.** Waiting for arc to be "done" defers the very venture that a third of arc's remaining plan library needs in order to be built at all.
- [N4] **The outside literature cannot rescue either side.** No study compares solo founders who tool-first against those who ship first; the four famous counter-cases are survivors with no visible denominator; the venture-studio statistics trace to one non-public report from a commercially interested body. This verdict rests on arc's own documents, which are High-confidence and one-directional — not on startup folklore.

DISSENT (strongest surviving opposing point):
- [A5] arc is architected as *shared* infrastructure for many future ventures, which is structurally unlike Slack, Trello, Rails or Shopify — each a single side-tool inside one already-operating business. Those cases therefore disprove a generic bootstrapping narrative without mapping cleanly onto arc's actual shape, and no controlled study fills that gap. This is a real limit on how far the evidence reaches. It is also, honestly, defensive only: it shows the counter-cases do not settle the question, never that arc-first is right.

CHEAPEST TEST TO DE-RISK:
- **Define one word, today: what "Cycle 2 closed" means.** It is a one-line decision costing minutes, and it resolves the single most decision-relevant ambiguity in the run — it fixes when the venture trigger fires and retroactively settles whether the four inserted cycles were compliant. Until that word is defined, both "the trigger has not fired" and "the trigger is overdue" remain arguable from the same text, which is precisely how a deferral becomes indefinite without anyone deciding to defer.
- Second, and nearly free: **"good shape" appears nowhere in the repo outside these council files.** If it is to govern a sequencing decision it needs a written, checkable definition — and per [N2] that definition cannot include the six venture-gated plans, or it can never be satisfied.
